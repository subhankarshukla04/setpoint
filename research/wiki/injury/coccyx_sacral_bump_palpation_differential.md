# Palpable bump near the coccyx — differential and when to refer

> **DISCLAIMER — not medical advice.** This article catalogues the common differential for a small palpable lump near the tailbone so a user can match their symptoms to a category and decide how urgently to see a clinician. It does **not** diagnose any individual case. Any lump that is changing, painful, discharging, or accompanied by red-flag symptoms requires in-person medical examination.

> A small soft bump near the coccyx in a young, otherwise-healthy lifter is most often a benign cyst, sinus, lipoma, or bony variant — but only an in-person exam can sort them, and a defined set of red flags warrants prompt referral.

## What the evidence says

The differential for a palpable lump in the sacrococcygeal region in a young adult is dominated by four benign categories. **Pilonidal disease** is the most common cause in men aged 15–30 and is closely associated with sitting, sweating, hair, and contact sport; the classic finding is one or more small midline pits in the gluteal cleft, sometimes with a slightly off-midline palpable bump or sinus tract that may intermittently discharge [^uptodate_pilonidal][^bmjbp_pilonidal][^nhs_pilonidal][^aafp_pilonidal]. **Epidermoid (sebaceous) cysts** are slow-growing, smooth, mobile subcutaneous nodules, often with a tiny central punctum, and can occur anywhere on the trunk including the buttock [^uptodate_epidermoid][^mayo_sebaceous]. **Lipomas** are soft, doughy, mobile, painless fatty masses [^aafp_lumps][^cleveland_lipoma]. A **bony variant of the coccyx tip** can simply palpate as a "bump" in lean people; the coccyx is normally 3–5 fused segments and frequently deviates slightly laterally as a normal anatomic variant [^statpearls_coccyx][^cleveland_coccyx].

Trauma sources matter in a footballer. A direct hit produces a contusion or hematoma that is tender, may discolour, and resolves in 2–4 weeks; a fall onto the tailbone can produce coccyx contusion or fracture with localised soft-tissue swelling [^cleveland_coccyx][^aafp_lumps]. Small overlying bursae can also become inflamed under repetitive pressure [^statpearls_coccyx].

The categories that genuinely need to be ruled out are uncommon but consequential: soft-tissue sarcomas have well-defined warning features — large (>5 cm), deep to fascia, hard, fixed, rapidly growing, or painful at night — any one of which warrants prompt imaging and specialist review [^uptodate_softtissue][^bmjbp_softtissue]. Long-standing untreated pilonidal sinus has a small malignancy risk; the clinical message is not to ignore a chronic discharging sinus for years [^bmjbp_pilonidal].

## Practical rules — match the pattern, decide the tier

**Likely benign (routine GP / walk-in within a few weeks):**
- Soft, mobile, painless, stable lump → fits epidermoid cyst or lipoma profile [^aafp_lumps][^uptodate_epidermoid].
- Hard, fixed but symmetric/midline, present "as long as I can remember," painless → fits coccyx tip / bony variant [^statpearls_coccyx].
- Recent contact-sport hit, tender, mild discolouration, settling over weeks → fits contusion [^aafp_lumps].

**Timely (1–2 weeks, book this week):**
- Visible midline pit or sinus opening near the cleft, intermittent discharge → likely pilonidal sinus [^uptodate_pilonidal][^nhs_pilonidal].
- Lump becoming red, hot, painful, or enlarging over days → possible cyst infection or pilonidal abscess [^aafp_pilonidal][^cleveland_pilonidal].

**Red flags — prompt referral within days, not weeks:**
- Rapid growth (visibly larger week-on-week, doubling within weeks) [^uptodate_softtissue][^bmjbp_softtissue].
- Hard, fixed (does not move under skin), >5 cm, or deep to fascia [^uptodate_softtissue].
- Skin breakdown — ulceration, bleeding, irregular dark pigmentation [^aafp_lumps].
- Fever, night sweats, unexplained weight loss [^lancet_redflags].
- Severe pain at night that wakes you from sleep [^uptodate_softtissue].
- Personal/family history of cancer with a new persistent lump [^lancet_redflags].

**Same-day ER:**
- Saddle anaesthesia (numbness around perineum / inner thighs), new bowel or bladder dysfunction, or progressive lower-limb weakness — this is cauda equina territory and is independent of the lump itself [^lancet_redflags].

## Self-assessment to do today
- Photograph with a ruler for scale, same lighting/angle weekly.
- Document: location (midline vs left/right of midline), distance from coccyx tip, mobility (does it slide under the skin?), texture (soft / firm / hard), tenderness (always vs only when pressed), any visible pit/punctum, any discharge.
- Track aggravators: sitting time, football contact, sweating.

## Training implications (until examined)
- Avoid direct mechanical pressure on it — leg-press footplate position, lying flat on it for ab work, deep-loaded squats if it is currently irritated.
- Football: a doughnut cushion on the bench between drives if pressure aggravates it.
- Lifting performance is unlikely to be affected unless the lump becomes inflamed.

## What this does not say
- This article is not a diagnosis. Distinguishing pilonidal sinus from an off-midline epidermoid cyst, in particular, requires inspection for a pit and is not reliably done by self-palpation.
- Absence of red flags here is not absence of pathology — every new lump deserves at least one in-person exam for documentation [^aafp_lumps].
- Imaging (ultrasound, MRI) is decided by the examining clinician, not by online checklists [^uptodate_softtissue].

## Refer out criteria
Book non-urgent care (walk-in or family doctor) for any new persistent lump even if it looks benign. Escalate within days if any red flag appears: rapid growth, hard/fixed/>5 cm, deep to fascia, skin ulceration or bleeding, fever / night sweats / weight loss, severe night pain, or personal history of cancer [^uptodate_softtissue][^bmjbp_softtissue][^lancet_redflags]. Go to the ER same day for saddle anaesthesia, bowel/bladder change, or progressive leg weakness [^lancet_redflags]. In Canada, telehealth (Telehealth Ontario, Maple, Telus Health) is a reasonable first triage from home for non-emergencies; ER for red flags only.
