# Golfer's elbow (medial epicondylitis) — self-managed rehab

> Stage isometrics → Tyler twist eccentrics → heavy slow resistance → return to lift, gated by the ≤3/10 pain rule. Skip cortisone. Expect 6–12 weeks. Refer out for nerve, instability, or no progress at 8 weeks.

## What the evidence says

Medial epicondylitis is a chronic tendinosis (degeneration, not inflammation) of the common flexor-pronator origin at the medial epicondyle. Pronator teres and flexor carpi radialis are the most commonly involved; palmaris longus, FCU and FDS share the origin. The classic exam finding is point tenderness 5–10 mm distal and anterior to the medial epicondyle with reproduction of pain on resisted wrist flexion and forearm pronation [^statpearls_medial][^physiopedia_medial]. Ulnar neuritis is concomitant in roughly 1 in 5 cases, so the ulnar nerve must be screened (Tinel sign, cubital tunnel compression) before treating purely as a tendon problem [^statpearls_medial].

Cook & Purdam's continuum model frames tendon pathology as reactive → disrepair → degenerative, with the early stages reversible under appropriate load — which is the basis of staged loading rather than rest [^cook_purdam_2009]. Two protocols anchor the evidence base. Rio 2015 demonstrated that 5×45 s isometric holds at ~70% MVIC produce immediate analgesia (≈6.8-point pain drop, lasting at least 45 min) with parallel reduction in cortical inhibition — the use case is symptom modulation in irritable presentations [^rio2015isometric]. Tyler 2014 showed that a 3×15 daily eccentric protocol with the red FlexBar ("Reverse Tyler Twist") significantly reduced pain and increased strength in chronic medial epicondylitis patients who had already failed standard care over 6 weeks [^tyler2014flexbar].

The Coombes 2013 RCT is the reason to refuse a cortisone shot if one is offered: short-term symptom relief, but worse 1-year recovery (83% vs 96%) and far higher recurrence (54% vs 12%) than placebo [^coombes2013steroid]. Passive-only modalities (ultrasound, massage, ice) and complete rest beyond 1–2 weeks similarly fail to remodel tendon and prolong symptoms [^osutendon][^cook_purdam_2009]. Realistic timeline with consistent, progressive loading is 6–12 weeks; cortisone history extends that to 3–6 months [^e3rehab_golfers].

## Practical rules

- 4-stage protocol — advance only when the pain rule holds for ~1–2 weeks.

| Stage | Criteria | Load | Sets/Reps | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 — irritable | Pain at rest or with daily use | Isometric wrist flexion at ~70% MVIC | 5×45 s | 1–2×/day |
| 1 — provoked only | Pain only with provocation | Tyler twist (red FlexBar) eccentrics + light DB wrist curls | Twist 3×15; curls 3×10–15 | Twist daily, curls 3×/wk |
| 2 — functional | Pain ≤2/10 in daily life | Heavy slow resistance: wrist curls, reverse curls, hammer curls, neutral-grip carries, pronation work | 3×6–10 (HSR tempo 3-1-3) | 2–3×/wk |
| 3 — return to lift | Pain-free under stage-2 load | Reintroduce chin-ups, deadlifts, rows; bias neutral grip + thicker handles | Standard programming | Per program |

- Tyler twist exact protocol: red 15 lb FlexBar, 3 sets of 15 eccentric wrist extensions on the symptomatic side, daily, 6–8 weeks [^tyler2014flexbar].
- Pain rules (Silbernagel/Stanish): ≤3/10 during work sets; not worse the next morning; morning stiffness not progressively worsening week-on-week. Violation → drop load 20% or revert one stage [^silbernagel2007pain].
- Modify (do not stop) while symptomatic: pull-ups → neutral grip + bands, volume −50%; rows → chest-supported, no underhand; barbell curls → swap to hammer/rope; bench → widen grip slightly, ease bar squeeze; deadlifts → straps; skull crushers → stay blocked (existing right-side rule).
- Refuse cortisone. Worse 1-year outcomes vs placebo [^coombes2013steroid].
- Screen ulnar nerve at every check-in: tap the cubital tunnel; if ring + little finger numbness appears, escalate.

## What this does not say

- This is a self-management protocol for a presentation that matches medial epicondylitis. It is not a diagnosis. If grip weakness, hand-muscle wasting, instability, night pain, fever, or trauma is present, the framework no longer applies.
- Eccentric vs isometric vs HSR supremacy is not settled for medial elbow specifically — most strong RCT data is from lateral elbow, patellar and Achilles tendons and extrapolated [^tyler2014flexbar][^rio2015isometric].
- Isometric analgesia is more reliably reproduced in lower-limb than upper-limb tendons — useful tool, not a guarantee [^rio2015isometric].
- Total rest is not the answer; tendons remodel under load [^cook_purdam_2009][^osutendon].

## Red flags / refer out

- Numbness or tingling in the ring + little finger, grip weakness, or hand-muscle wasting → ulnar neuropathy / cubital tunnel; escalate (urgent care, walk-in, telehealth) [^johnshopkins_cubital][^orthoinfo_cubital].
- Acute pop with valgus stress, instability under load, or medial bruising → suspect UCL injury [^statpearls_medial].
- Night pain unrelated to position, fever, palpable mass/lump, or recent fall/trauma → not tendinopathy; needs imaging.
- No measurable improvement after 8 weeks of compliant, progressive loading → escalate to sports-med physician [^e3rehab_golfers][^cook_purdam_2009].
