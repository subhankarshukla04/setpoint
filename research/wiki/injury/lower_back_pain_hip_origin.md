# Lower back pain of hip origin

> Most "low back pain" in young lifters who sit a lot is the lumbar spine borrowing range that the hips can't deliver — restore hip extension and internal rotation, drill the hinge, and the back stops complaining.

## What the evidence says

The hip-spine relationship is bidirectional: when the hip joint cannot produce the ROM a task demands, the lumbar spine "borrows" the missing degrees. Walking, deadlifting, sport rotation — every movement budget is split between hip and lumbar; if the hip is short, the lumbar pays [^mcgill_back_mechanic][^sahrmann_msi]. This is why someone who "can't tell" if their back is the problem feels relief from hip drills: the hip drills are reducing the spine's compensatory workload, not treating the spine directly.

The popular "anterior pelvic tilt causes back pain" story is weaker than commonly claimed. Resting APT differs by only ~2–3° between asymptomatic and symptomatic populations — not a clinical signal [^laird2014apt][^herrington2011apt]. The better framing is mechanical: tight hip flexors and a stiff posterior hip capsule limit hip extension, so during gait and standing the lumbar segments extend to keep the trunk upright. Repeated thousands of times per day, that is the load that hurts. Similarly, hip internal rotation deficit forces compensatory lumbar rotation in the squat, lunge, and rotational sport — and the lumbar spine collectively allows only ~10–13° of rotation [^vanwingerden2012hipir].

For non-specific chronic low back pain (the vast majority of cases per the Lancet 2018 series), first-line care is reassurance, staying active, and exercise — any modality the patient will adhere to [^lancet2018_1][^lancet2018_2]. McGill's "Big 3" (curl-up, side bridge, bird dog) build endurance-based co-contraction of the trunk wall without spinal compression and remain the most widely cited evidence-supported routine [^mcgill_big3][^bjsm_lbp_2018]. Hip mobility drills (90/90, couch stretch, world's greatest stretch) produce acute ROM gains and, paired with motor-control retraining of the hip hinge, reduce the lumbar's compensatory burden during loaded patterns [^physiotutors_hip][^e3rehab_lbp].

## Practical rules

- Suspect hip-driven LBP if: you sit >6 hrs/day, hip flexors feel tight, hip mobility drills give symptomatic relief, pain is dull/achy and load-related (not radiating, not constant).
- Daily 10-minute protocol (do it before training, or first thing in the morning):
  1. 90/90 hip stretch — 2 min each side [^physiotutors_hip].
  2. Couch stretch — 90 s each side [^e3rehab_lbp].
  3. McGill Big 3 — curl-up 2×10, side plank 2×30 s/side, bird dog 2×10/side [^mcgill_big3].
  4. World's greatest stretch — 5 reps per side [^barbellrehab_lbp].
- BIAS these patterns: hip thrusts (glute strength = hip extension) [^contreras_thrust]; Romanian deadlifts (grooves the hinge); walking lunges (loaded hip extension); goblet squats (upright torso); farmer + suitcase carries (anti-flexion / anti-lateral-flexion endurance) [^mcgill_back_mechanic].
- AUDIT during a flare: conventional deadlift → trap bar or RDL until hinge is clean; back squat → lighter, brace hard, no lumbar tuck; bent-over row → chest-supported row; strict OHP → ribs-down, glutes squeezed, no rib flare [^barbellrehab_lbp][^e3rehab_lbp].
- Pain rule: keep training pain ≤3/10, no worse the next morning. If a lift violates this for 2+ sessions, swap the variant.

## What this does not say

- This is not a substitute for diagnosis. "Hip-origin LBP" is a pattern, not a tissue source. Persistent pain >6–8 weeks of structured loading deserves a hands-on assessment.
- APT itself is not a disease. Cosmetic pelvic position changes are not a treatment goal; restoring hip extension and bracing capacity are [^laird2014apt][^herrington2011apt].
- Stretching alone does not fix this. The compensation is motor-pattern as much as tissue-length; the McGill Big 3 + loaded hinge work is what re-distributes load away from the lumbar [^mcgill_big3][^hodges_motor_control].
- Total rest is the wrong default. Lancet and BJSM consensus is staying active beats bed rest for nearly all non-specific LBP [^lancet2018_1][^bjsm_lbp_2018].

## Red flags / refer out

- Pain radiating below the knee, numbness/tingling in the leg → suspect radiculopathy, refer [^lancet2018_1].
- Saddle anaesthesia, bowel or bladder changes → cauda equina, ER same day [^lancet2018_1].
- Night pain that wakes from sleep, unexplained weight loss, fever, history of cancer → systemic disease workup [^lancet2018_2].
- Acute trauma in osteoporosis-risk individuals → fracture screen [^bjsm_lbp_2018].
- No improvement after 6–8 weeks of structured mobility + loading → physio referral [^bjsm_lbp_2018].
