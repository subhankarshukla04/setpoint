# Medial elbow valgus extension overload

> Most "valgus overload" symptoms in lifters are actually triceps/flexor-pronator tendinopathy — load progressively, keep pain ≤3/10, and refer out for nerve, locking, or instability signs.

## What the evidence says

Valgus extension overload (VEO) is a thrower's syndrome: repeated valgus stress with hyperextension drives posteromedial olecranon impingement, osteophyte formation, and chronic UCL strain [^ahmad2010veo][^radiopaedia_veo]. Pure VEO is uncommon in lifters because barbell pressing does not produce the late-cocking valgus moment that throwing does, and the UCL itself rarely limits non-throwing weight training [^johnshopkinsucl]. When a lifter complains of medial elbow pain after skull crushers, JM press, or close-grip bench, the most likely diagnoses are distal triceps tendinopathy and medial epicondylitis (flexor-pronator tendinopathy), not UCL injury [^statpearls_medial][^e3rehab_triceps].

That said, the UCL and the flexor-pronator mass share load: the UCL anterior band is the primary static valgus stabilizer and the flexor carpi ulnaris is the primary dynamic stabilizer [^pmc_fcu][^physio_elbow_lig]. Strengthening wrist flexion and pronation reduces ligamentous demand even when the complaint is "tendon-like." Radiology reviews note "weightlifter's elbow" is sometimes a missed posteromedial impingement or olecranon stress reaction, so persistent pain with mechanical symptoms deserves imaging rather than another deload [^auntminnie_weightlifter].

Tendons require progressive load to remodel; full unloading worsens degeneration [^osutendon]. The current evidence-based stack is: isometrics for early pain modulation (5×45 s at ~70% MVC, 2×/day), then heavy slow resistance (HSR) or eccentric work, with pain ≤3/10 during and 24 h after the session as the gate for progression [^pmc_isometric][^cardoso2022upperlimb][^matthews2020eccentric]. Eccentric and HSR protocols both outperform passive treatment for elbow tendinopathy in meta-analyses [^matthews2020eccentric].

Realistic timeline is 3+ months to full resolution; symptom duration shortens with adherence to progressive loading and worsens with the rest-then-test cycle most lifters default to [^peakphysio_triceps][^e3rehab_triceps].

## Practical rules

- Permanent (while symptomatic) block list: skull crushers, JM press, overhead DB triceps extension — these max-load the triceps tendon in lengthened position [^e3rehab_triceps][^aworkout_skulls].
- Pain monitor: ≤3/10 during work sets, no worse the next morning. Violation → drop load 20% or swap exercise [^cardoso2022upperlimb].
- Rebuild ladder (advance only when pain rule holds for ~1–2 weeks):
  1. Isometric extension at 90°, 5×45 s @ ~70% effort, 2×/day [^pmc_isometric].
  2. Rope pushdowns, 3×8–12, 3-1-3 tempo, mid-range [^e3rehab_triceps].
  3. Close-grip bench at partial ROM (stop at ~90° elbow flexion) [^peakphysio_triceps].
  4. Full-ROM pressing, widened grip, elbows ~45–60° from torso [^barbellrehab_bench].
- Bench cues: retract scap, grip ~70–75% biacromial width, tucked elbows offload medial structures [^barbellrehab_bench].
- Add flexor-pronator work 2–3×/week (light wrist flexion curls + DB pronation) — dynamic UCL support [^pmc_fcu].
- Expect 8–16 weeks back to pre-injury loads. >12 weeks without progress → sports-med referral [^peakphysio_triceps][^osutendon].

## What this does not say

- This is not a diagnosis. "Medial elbow valgus overload" is shorthand here for a presentation pattern; the actual tissue source (triceps tendon, flexor-pronator, UCL, olecranon) needs clinical exam.
- Evidence is strongest for patellar/Achilles and lateral elbow tendinopathy; elbow-specific RCTs are smaller and protocols are extrapolated [^osutendon][^matthews2020eccentric].
- "Permanent skull-crusher block" is a personal-history rule for an injured user, not a population claim — asymptomatic lifters can train them with proper loading [^aworkout_skulls].
- Total rest is not the answer; deconditioning prolongs recovery [^osutendon].

## Red flags / refer out

- Numbness/tingling in ring + little finger, grip weakness, or hand muscle wasting → ulnar neuropathy / cubital tunnel; refer if >6 weeks or any motor loss [^johnshopkins_cubital][^orthoinfo_cubital].
- Palpable nerve snap over medial epicondyle with elbow flexion = ulnar nerve subluxation (documented in lifters with hypertrophic medial triceps) [^pmc_ulnar_disloc].
- Mechanical locking, catching, or hard block to terminal extension → suspect loose body / posteromedial impingement; imaging needed [^ahmad2010veo][^auntminnie_weightlifter].
- Acute pop with valgus stress, medial bruising, or instability under load → suspect UCL sprain/tear [^johnshopkinsucl].
- No improvement after 6–8 weeks of structured progressive loading → sports-med physician [^osutendon].
