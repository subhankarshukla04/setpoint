# Prophylactic joint health for lifters

> The strongest "prehab" is sustainable exercise selection and volume, not a band routine — strength training itself cuts injury risk by roughly half.

## What the evidence says

Lauersen's BJSM meta-analysis of randomised trials found structured strength programs reduced acute sports injuries by ~one-third and overuse injuries by ~half, with a dose-response — every 10% increase in training load lowered injury risk further [^lauersen2014][^lauersen2018]. The most useful reframing for a 4-day-split lifter: prehab is not a separate ten-minute band routine, it is choosing exercises and volumes you can repeat for years without joint flare-ups [^bbm_prehab][^e3rehab_prehab]. The supported preventive stack is small, joint-specific, and dosable.

For knees, the evidence supports tibialis anterior loading, full-ROM single-leg work, and heavy slow resistance for the patellar tendon [^malliaras2015hsr][^kongsgaard2009][^bbm_knees]. For shoulders, the most-cited prehab moves are face pulls and band/cable external rotation, plus tucked-elbow bench mechanics; the underlying RCT base is rotator-cuff strengthening reducing impingement-type pain [^kuhn2009rc][^jobe1991][^sbs_shoulders]. For the spine, McGill's "Big 3" plus loaded carries build anti-extension/anti-rotation endurance with minimal compressive cost [^mcgill2007][^mcgill_carries]. Static stretching pre-lift is not part of the picture: it offers no injury benefit and can transiently reduce force [^behm2016stretch].

Volume caps matter. Practitioner-curated review of survey data and lifting-cohort literature points to horizontal pressing >16 hard sets/week as a shoulder-symptom risk and a pull:push ratio of at least 1:1 (often 2:1 for desk workers) as protective [^sbs_shoulders][^mass_pullpush].

## Practical rules

### Knees
- Tibialis anterior raises 2×/week, 2–3×15–25 [^bbm_knees].
- One loaded single-leg exercise weekly: split squat / reverse lunge / step-up, 2–3×6–12/leg [^e3rehab_knees].
- Train full ROM (ATG) when pain-free; regress to partial ROM on irritated days, do not skip [^malliaras2015hsr].
- If patellar-tendon-prone: heavy slow resistance squats (3–4×6–8, 6 s tempo) or Spanish squats 2×/week [^kongsgaard2009].
- Cap jumping volume during deficit weeks [^osutendon].

### Shoulders
- Face pulls 2–3×/week, 3 sets of 12–20 [^sbs_shoulders].
- Light external rotation (band/cable) 2×/week, 2×12–15 [^kuhn2009rc][^jobe1991].
- Skip behind-the-neck pressing; default to landmine and DB OHP [^bbm_prehab].
- Bench: scap retract+depress, elbows tucked ~30–45° from torso, not flared 90° [^barbellrehab_bench].
- Rotate horizontal pressing variant every 4–8 weeks (incline / flat / dip).
- Cap horizontal pressing at 12–16 hard sets/week; pull:push ≥ 1:1 (2:1 for desk workers) [^sbs_shoulders][^mass_pullpush].

### Lower back
- McGill Big 3 daily, 5–8 reps × 3 sets, descending hold time [^mcgill2007].
- Loaded carries weekly — farmer + suitcase, 3 sets × 30–60 s [^mcgill_carries].
- Substitute hip thrusts for heavy back squats on flare-up weeks (comparable glute output, lower spinal load) [^contreras_thrust].
- Trap-bar deadlift as default heavy hinge — lower lumbar moment than conventional [^swinton2011].

### Elbows
- Wrist curls + reverse curls 1–2×/week, 2×12–15 — already protective for heavy pullers and pressers.
- FlexBar Tyler twist 2×/week even pre-symptom for heavy-pulling lifters [^tyler2010].
- Vary grip width across pressing sessions [^barbellrehab_bench].

### Warm-up
- 5 min general aerobic + 2–3 ramp sets on the first compound of a body part. No static stretching pre-lift [^behm2016stretch].

### Programming hygiene
- Change one programming variable at a time (load, volume, frequency, exercise selection). When pain shows up, you can attribute it [^bbm_prehab].
- Tag exercises in the planner as `prophylactic`, `joint-conservative`, or `joint-aggressive`. Default plans should include ≥1 prophylactic exercise per body part per week.

## What this does not say

- Prehab does not substitute for managing total fatigue; volume creep with no deloads will still injure you regardless of band work [^bbm_prehab]. See `deload_triggers`.
- The "Knees Over Toes" branding is louder than its evidence; the underlying ingredients (tib loading, ATG split squats, slant-board work) are supported, the specific sequence is not RCT-validated [^bbm_knees].
- Face pulls and external rotation work prevent symptoms in overhead athletes by RCT; effect size in asymptomatic recreational lifters is extrapolated [^kuhn2009rc].
- These rules are for asymptomatic prevention. If you already have joint pain, switch to a rehab-decision article — see `training_in_pain_decision_framework`.

## Red flags / refer out

- Sharp, mechanical knee catching/locking, or knee giving way under load → meniscal/ligamentous; refer.
- Shoulder pain at rest or at night, painful arc with weakness in external rotation against resistance → suspect rotator-cuff tear; refer if persists >4–6 weeks [^kuhn2009rc].
- Low back pain with leg numbness, foot drop, saddle anaesthesia, or bowel/bladder change → emergency referral.
- Elbow numbness/tingling in ring + little finger → see `medial_elbow_valgus_overload` for cubital-tunnel red flags.
- Any joint pain that is unchanged or worsening after 6–8 weeks of structured loading + modification → sports-med MD or physio.
