# Tennis elbow (lateral epicondylitis) — self-managed rehab

> Stage isometrics → Tyler twist eccentrics (green → blue → black FlexBar) → heavy slow resistance → return to lift, gated by the ≤3/10 pain rule. Skip cortisone (worse 1-year outcomes than doing nothing). Expect 6–12 weeks; full recurrence-free recovery 6–12 months under load.

## What the evidence says

Lateral epicondylitis is a chronic tendinosis (degeneration, not inflammation) of the common extensor origin at the lateral epicondyle, with the **extensor carpi radialis brevis (ECRB)** the primary culprit; extensor digitorum communis and ECRL are secondary contributors. The classic exam is point tenderness 5–10 mm distal and anterior to the lateral epicondyle, reproduced by **Cozen's test** (resisted wrist extension with the elbow extended) and **Maudsley's test** (resisted middle-finger extension) [^statpearls_lateral][^physiopedia_lateral]. The radial nerve / posterior interosseous nerve runs through the radial tunnel adjacent to the ECRB and must be screened — radial tunnel syndrome mimics lateral epicondylitis but the tender point sits ~4 cm distal to the lateral epicondyle and provocation comes from resisted supination, not wrist extension [^statpearls_lateral].

Cook & Purdam's continuum model (reactive → disrepair → degenerative) is the basis for staged loading rather than rest [^cook_purdam_2009]. Three protocols anchor the evidence base. **Tyler 2010** (J Shoulder Elbow Surg) is the seminal RCT: adding the FlexBar eccentric "Tyler Twist" (3×15 daily) to standard PT in chronic lateral epicondylosis produced an 81% improvement in pain and 72% improvement in strength at 7 weeks vs no clinically meaningful change in the control group — effect size large enough that the trial was halted early on ethical grounds [^tyler2010flexbar]. **Peterson 2014** confirmed eccentric > concentric loading for the same population [^peterson2014eccentric]. **Rio 2015** isometric work (5×45 s at ~70% MVIC) produces immediate analgesia in irritable presentations [^rio2015isometric]. The **Cullinane 2014 systematic review** of 12 trials concluded eccentric exercise is the single highest-grade intervention for chronic lateral epicondylitis [^cullinane2014systematic].

The case against cortisone is unusually strong here. **Bisset 2006 (BMJ)** randomised 198 patients to wait-and-see, PT, or corticosteroid: steroid had the best 6-week outcomes but the **worst** 52-week outcomes (68% recovered vs 94% for PT vs 90% for wait-and-see), with high recurrence [^bisset2006bmj]. **Coombes 2013 (JAMA)** replicated this — steroid worse 1-year recovery (83% vs 96%) and far higher recurrence (54% vs 12%) than placebo [^coombes2013steroid]. **Smidt 2002 (Lancet)** showed the same ranking earlier [^smidt2002lancet]. Passive-only modalities (ultrasound, massage, ice) and total rest beyond 1–2 weeks do not remodel tendon and prolong symptoms [^osutendon][^cook_purdam_2009]. Realistic compliant timeline is 6–12 weeks to functional, 6–12 months to recurrence-free under heavy load [^e3rehab_tennis][^stasinopoulos2017].

## Practical rules

- 4-stage protocol — advance only when the pain rule holds for ~1–2 weeks.

| Stage | Criteria | Load | Sets/Reps | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 — irritable | Pain at rest or with daily use, grip painful unloaded | Isometric wrist extension at ~70% MVIC | 5×45 s | 1–2×/day |
| 1 — provoked only | Pain only with provocation | Tyler twist (green FlexBar, 6 lb) eccentrics + light DB reverse wrist curls | Twist 3×15; reverse curls 3×10–15 | Twist daily, curls 3×/wk |
| 2 — functional | Pain ≤2/10 in daily life | Heavy slow resistance: reverse wrist curls, hammer curls, supination/pronation work, **thick-grip** carries; progress FlexBar to blue (15 lb) then black (25 lb) | 3×6–10 (HSR tempo 3-1-3) | 2–3×/wk |
| 3 — return to lift | Pain-free under stage-2 load | Reintroduce pull-ups, deadlifts, rows, curls; bias **neutral grip** + thicker handles to redistribute load off ECRB | Standard programming | Per program |

- **Tyler twist exact protocol (Tyler 2010):** start with the **green** FlexBar (6 lb), 3 sets of 15 eccentric wrist *extensions* on the symptomatic side, daily, 6–8 weeks. Progress to blue (15 lb) when current bar is pain-free at 3×15. Mechanism: load the affected wrist into extension with the healthy hand, then slowly resist as the affected hand returns to flexion — eccentric load on ECRB [^tyler2010flexbar].
- **Pain rules (Silbernagel/Stanish):** ≤3/10 during work sets; not worse the next morning; morning stiffness not progressively worsening week-on-week. Violation → drop load 20% or revert one stage [^silbernagel2007pain].
- **Modify (do not stop) while symptomatic:** pull-ups → neutral grip + bands, volume −50%; rows → chest-supported, neutral grip; barbell curls → swap to hammer/rope; bench → loosen the death-grip, slight wider grip; deadlifts → straps; **drop reverse-grip pulldowns and supinated chins entirely** until stage 2; tricep extensions stay (don't load the lateral epicondyle).
- **Refuse cortisone.** Worse 1-year outcomes vs placebo and vs doing nothing; high recurrence [^bisset2006bmj][^coombes2013steroid][^smidt2002lancet].
- **Aggravator audit:** thin grips, racquet-sport backhand mechanics, mouse position with extended wrist, prolonged keyboard with wrist hyperextension. Thick handles distribute force; a vertical mouse and neutral keyboard tray are non-trivial wins.
- **Counterforce ("tennis elbow") brace** can help short-term during provocative tasks by shifting load distal to the tendon; do not use it as a substitute for loading [^physiopedia_lateral].
- **Screen the radial nerve** at every check-in: if symptoms include forearm fatigue with sustained pronation, weakness in finger extension, or tenderness 4 cm distal to the lateral epicondyle (not at the bone) — suspect radial tunnel and escalate.

## What this does not say

- This is a self-management protocol for a presentation that matches lateral epicondylitis. It is not a diagnosis. Grip weakness, hand-muscle wasting, instability, night pain, fever, or trauma → framework no longer applies.
- Eccentric loading has the strongest RCT base for lateral elbow, but isometric vs eccentric vs HSR supremacy is not fully settled — most lateral elbow RCT data favours eccentrics; isometric analgesia is more reliably reproduced in lower-limb tendons [^rio2015isometric][^cullinane2014systematic].
- PRP, prolotherapy, and shockwave have mixed evidence; not first-line, not a substitute for progressive loading [^stasinopoulos2017].
- Total rest is not the answer; tendons remodel under load [^cook_purdam_2009][^osutendon].

## Red flags / refer out

- Numbness/tingling in the dorsum of the hand or thumb-side fingers, weakness extending the fingers/thumb, or tenderness 4 cm distal to the lateral epicondyle → radial tunnel / posterior interosseous neuropathy; escalate.
- Acute pop with varus stress, instability under load, or lateral bruising → suspect lateral collateral ligament (LCL) injury.
- Night pain unrelated to position, fever, palpable mass, or trauma → not tendinopathy; needs imaging.
- No measurable improvement after 8 weeks of compliant, progressive loading → escalate to sports-med physician [^e3rehab_tennis][^cook_purdam_2009].
