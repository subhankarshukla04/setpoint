# Training-in-pain decision framework

> Pain ≤5/10 during, back to baseline within 24 h, no week-on-week worsening — train through it. Anything else, modify or skip. Complete rest is almost never the right answer.

## What the evidence says

The shift from "rest until pain-free" to "load while monitoring pain" is one of the better-supported reversals in sports medicine. Stanish's 1986 eccentric protocol first showed that controlled loading-while-painful beat rest for chronic tendinopathy [^stanish1986]; Cook & Purdam's continuum model later reframed tendinopathy as a load-management problem at every stage — reactive, disrepair, or degenerative — with isometric loading early, isotonic mid, and heavy-slow or eccentric work late [^cook_purdam2009][^cook2016continuum]. The 2020 ICON consensus on tendinopathy terminology endorses progressive loading as the cornerstone treatment and warns explicitly against complete rest beyond 7–10 days, which deconditions the tendon and prolongs recovery [^scott2020icon].

The operational rule across this literature is the Silbernagel pain-monitoring model, originally developed for Achilles tendinopathy and now widely generalised: pain during loading is acceptable up to ~5/10, pain should return to baseline within 24 h, morning stiffness should not increase week-on-week, and performance should not decline week-on-week [^silbernagel2007][^silbernagel2019]. Violating any of the four rules means the prior session exceeded tolerable dose — regress, don't quit. Rio's work adds a tactical tool: a single bout of 5×45 s high-load isometric at ~70% MVIC produces immediate analgesia lasting roughly 45 minutes, useful as a pre-training pain modulator [^rio2015isometric][^rio2017infographic].

Modern pain neuroscience reframes the broader picture. The IASP 2020 redefinition treats pain as a protective output of the nervous system shaped by context and expectation, not a one-to-one readout of tissue damage [^iasp2020][^moseley_explain_pain]. Fear-avoidance — catastrophising, hypervigilance, activity avoidance — predicts long-term disability in chronic musculoskeletal pain better than imaging does [^vlaeyen2000fa][^lethem1983fear]. Graded exposure under control reduces sensitisation; pulling back entirely reinforces it.

## Practical rules

- Silbernagel four-gate check (apply for 24 h after every session): pain during work sets ≤5/10 (conservative ≤3/10), pain at 24 h ≤ pre-session baseline, morning stiffness not increasing week-on-week, performance not declining week-on-week. Any violation → regress load 20–40% next session [^silbernagel2007].
- Traffic-light protocol per movement, per session:
  - **GREEN** (no pain or ≤2/10 during, no carryover) → proceed as planned.
  - **YELLOW** (3–5/10 during, mild carryover resolving <24 h) → MODIFY: load −20–40%, reduce ROM, reduce volume, 3-s eccentric tempo, switch grip/stance, swap to machine, or substitute isometric.
  - **RED** (>5/10 during, OR worse at 24 h, OR new neurological signs) → SKIP that movement; reassess; refer if persists [^e3rehab_pain][^bbm_painrules].
- Modification toolbox (least → most disruptive): load reduction → ROM reduction → volume reduction → tempo slowdown → variant swap → machine swap → isometric substitute [^cook_purdam2009][^rio2015isometric].
- Pre-training analgesia: 5×45 s isometric of the affected muscle at ~70% effort to reduce pain in the subsequent session [^rio2015isometric].
- Two-week rule: yellow without measurable improvement for 14 days → escalate (more aggressive modification, deload week, or refer out) [^silbernagel2019].
- Discriminate discomfort from injury pain: training discomfort is diffuse, effort-related, fatigue-onset, resolves <48 h; injury pain is sharp, well-localised at a joint or tendon insertion, present on first reps before fatigue, lingers >48 h [^scott2020icon].
- "Modify, don't skip" default: most pain points respond better to dose reduction than to complete rest; >7–10 days off deconditions the tendon [^scott2020icon][^cook2016continuum].

## What this does not say

- The ≤5/10 rule is for chronic, gradual-onset, soft-tissue pain — not for acute trauma, locking joints, or new neurological signs. Those go straight to RED [^scott2020icon].
- Pain is real even when imaging is clean. Pain neuroscience does not mean "it's in your head"; it means tissue state is one of many inputs the nervous system uses to produce pain [^iasp2020][^moseley_explain_pain].
- The 5/10 vs 3/10 threshold is convention; no head-to-head RCT cleanly differentiates them. Use 3/10 if you tend to push, 5/10 if you tend to bail too early.
- Graded exposure ≠ "ignore it and grind." It requires regression, monitoring, and a clear progression path back to full load [^vlaeyen2000fa].

## Red flags / refer out

- Neurological signs (numbness, tingling, weakness, dermatomal pattern).
- Night pain that wakes from sleep; unexplained weight loss; fever; malaise.
- Trauma with inability to bear weight or use the limb.
- Saddle anaesthesia, bowel or bladder changes → cauda equina, ER same day.
- No measurable improvement after 6–8 weeks of compliant graded loading.
- History of cancer, IV drug use, or immunosuppression with new spinal pain.
