# Creatine, Caffeine, and Vitamin D — Supplement Evidence

> Run 5 g creatine monohydrate daily, 3–6 mg/kg caffeine pre-training (never within 8 h of bed), and 2000 IU/day vitamin D3 in Toronto winters; skip everything else.

## Bottom line

Three supplements have defensible Tier-1 evidence for a natural lifter:
**creatine monohydrate** (the most-validated supplement, period),
**caffeine** (modest acute strength/power boost, sleep cost), and
**vitamin D** (only meaningful if blood levels are low — a real concern
for Toronto lifters Oct–Mar). Everything else is either marginal,
deficiency-conditional, or marketing.

## 1. Creatine monohydrate

### Protocol

- **3–5 g/day, daily, indefinitely.** Time of day is irrelevant over
  weeks [^kreider2017].
- **Loading is optional.** 20 g/day × 5–7 days reaches plateau in ~1
  week; 3–5 g/day reaches the same plateau in ~3–4 weeks. Skip the load
  to avoid GI discomfort.
- **Form: monohydrate.** "Buffered", "HCl", "ethyl ester", "magnesium
  chelate" forms are not superior and cost more [^jagim2018].

### Effects

| Outcome | Effect vs placebo | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Strength gain (6–12 wk) | +5–15% | Lanhers 2015, Kreider 2017 |
| Hypertrophy (8–12 wk) | +1–2 kg LBM | Chilibeck 2017 |
| Repeat-effort output | Moderate ↑ | ISSN 2017 |
| Cognition (sleep-deprived/vegetarian) | Small ↑ | Avgerinos 2018 |

### Cutting-relevant flags

- **Initial water-weight gain ~0.8–1.5 kg** (intramuscular water, **not**
  subcutaneous bloat). Bake into scale-trend smoothing in week 2.
- **No fat-loss interaction.** Continue through the cut.
- **Long-term safety established** at ≥5 years daily use [^kreider2017].
  Plasma creatinine assay artefact — flag to clinicians before bloodwork.

## 2. Caffeine

### Protocol

- **3–6 mg/kg, ~30–60 min pre-training** [^grgic2018][^grgic2020].
  80 kg lifter → 240–480 mg.
- Training days only; skip on rest days to preserve sensitivity.

### Effects

- **Strength: +2–7% 1RM**, larger on upper-body and lesser-trained
  subjects.
- **Power / bar velocity / jumps**: small-to-moderate ↑.
- **RPE**: lowers perceived effort — adherence aid in a deficit.

### Sleep & tolerance

- **Half-life ~5 h, full clearance 8–10 h.** Hard rule: **no caffeine
  within 8–10 h of bedtime** [^drake2013]. Training after ~2 PM with
  3+ mg/kg measurably degrades sleep latency and deep sleep.
- **Tolerance**: partial blunting within 2–4 weeks of daily use, but
  effect does not vanish. Cycle (1–2 weeks off every 2–3 months) **only
  if** diminished response is perceived.

## 3. Vitamin D

### When it matters

Supplementation only meaningfully moves training/health outcomes when
**baseline 25(OH)D is low**. Toronto sits at ~43.7°N — cutaneous
synthesis is effectively zero **October through March**. ~30–40% of
Canadians fall below 50 nmol/L in winter [^pilz2011][^holick2011]. A
22-year-old indoor-dwelling Toronto lifter is a high-prior candidate for
insufficiency.

### Cutoffs (serum 25(OH)D)

| Status | nmol/L |
|---|---|
| Deficient | <30 |
| Insufficient | 30–50 |
| Sufficient | 75–125 |

### Effects

- **Strength**: meta-analyses show benefit from **correcting deficiency**
  — small/null in already-replete subjects [^beaudart2014][^tomlinson2015].
- **Testosterone**: Pilz 2011 showed +25% T after 1 year of 3,332 IU/day
  in **deficient** men [^pilz2011]. Replete subjects show **no T effect**
  [^lopez2013]. The "vitamin D boosts testosterone" claim is overstated
  — it is deficiency correction, not augmentation.

### Dose

- **Maintenance: 1000–2000 IU/day** with a fat-containing meal.
- **Toronto default Oct–Mar: 2000 IU/day.**
- **If deficient** (<50 nmol/L): 4000–5000 IU/day × 8–12 weeks, retest,
  drop to maintenance.
- UL 4000 IU/day (Health Canada/IOM); toxicity rare below 10,000 IU/day
  chronic.

## 4. Secondary supplements (modest, conditional)

- **Omega-3 (EPA+DHA), 2–3 g/day** — modest soreness reduction; small
  MPS-sensitivity benefit in older adults; cardiovascular insurance
  [^jakeman2017][^smith2011]. Reasonable if fish intake is low.
- **Magnesium 200–400 mg/day (glycinate/citrate)** — ergogenic only if
  deficient; soft signal for sleep quality. Avoid oxide (poor
  bioavailability).
- **Vitamin C, zinc** — no ergogenic effect at sufficiency. **High-dose
  vitamin C around training may blunt adaptation** [^paulsen2014] — do
  not megadose.

## 5. No-evidence list (skip)

- **BCAAs** — redundant against any high-protein diet [^wolfe2017].
- **Glutamine** — not ergogenic in healthy lifters [^gleeson2008].
- **"Testosterone boosters"** (tribulus, fenugreek, D-aspartic acid, ZMA,
  most ashwagandha claims) — no clinically meaningful T effect in
  eugonadal men.
- **Fat burners** (yohimbine, synephrine, thermogenic blends) — effect
  is mostly the caffeine; side-effect profile not worth it.
- **Pre-workout proprietary blends** — caffeine + beta-alanine +
  citrulline at premium markup. Dose individually if at all.
- **Collagen for hypertrophy** — incomplete amino profile; whey/casein
  beat it.
- **Tart cherry, ZMA, "recovery blends"** — trivial effects. Sleep beats
  supplements for recovery.

## Engine rules

- `R-creatine-default`: 5 g monohydrate/day in every cutting plan unless
  contraindicated; smooth out the ~1 kg water-weight bump in week 2.
- `R-caffeine-window`: cap at 6 mg/kg; refuse to recommend caffeine
  within 8 h of user's logged bedtime.
- `R-vitamin-d-toronto`: 2000 IU/day default; flag user to test 25(OH)D;
  bump to 4000 IU/day × 12 weeks if <50 nmol/L.
- `R-skip-list`: actively recommend against BCAAs, glutamine,
  T-boosters, fat burners.

## Confidence

**High** for the three core supplements. Creatine and caffeine evidence
bases are among the strongest in sports nutrition; vitamin D claims are
high-confidence specifically for the **deficiency-correction** framing
and overstated everywhere else.

## Tier counts

- **T1: 8** (Kreider 2017 ISSN, ISSN 2017 position stand, Lanhers 2015,
  Grgic 2018, Grgic 2020, Pilz 2011, Lopez 2013, Holick 2011 Endocrine
  Society guideline).
- **T2: 3** (MASS Research Review, Stronger By Science guides,
  Examine.com — commercial, flagged).
- **T3: 1** (practitioner posts: Lyle McDonald, Helms — used only for
  cross-checking practical dosing).

## Recommendation — 22-year-old natural lifter cutting in Toronto

- **Creatine monohydrate, 5 g/day, every day, indefinitely.** The single
  most-validated supplement; expect ~1 kg water-weight in week 2 and
  ignore it.
- **Caffeine, 3–5 mg/kg ~45 min pre-training, training days only, never
  within 8 h of bed.** ~240–400 mg for an 80 kg lifter; cycle only if
  response visibly fades.
- **Vitamin D3, 2000 IU/day with a fat-containing meal, year-round
  (non-negotiable Oct–Mar).** Test 25(OH)D once — if <50 nmol/L, run
  4000 IU/day × 12 weeks then drop back. Skip every other shelf item.
