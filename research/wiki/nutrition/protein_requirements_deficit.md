# Protein Requirements in a Caloric Deficit

> Hold protein at 1.8–2.4 g/kg total body weight in any cut, spread across 3–5 feedings of 0.4 g/kg each to protect lean mass.

## Bottom line

Lifters cutting on a caloric deficit need **more** protein than at
maintenance, not less. The defensible Tier-1 range is **1.6–2.4 g/kg of
total body weight per day**, with leaner trainees pushing toward the top
of that band. Helms' 2014 systematic review caps the practical ceiling at
**2.3–3.1 g/kg of fat-free mass** for natural bodybuilders preparing for
contest [^helms2014][^phillips2014].

## Why intake rises in a deficit

Energy restriction increases amino-acid oxidation and blunts the muscle
protein synthesis (MPS) response to feeding. To preserve lean body mass
(LBM), dietary protein must compensate. Higher intakes also raise the
thermic effect of food (TEF ~20–30%) and improve satiety — both
adherence levers in a cut [^aragon2017].

Longland's 2016 RCT made the mechanism concrete: a 40% deficit with six
weekly resistance sessions produced **+1.2 kg LBM and −4.8 kg fat** on
2.4 g/kg/day, vs lean-mass stagnation on 1.2 g/kg/day [^longland2016].

## Total daily target

| Lifter profile | Target (g/kg total BW) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Maintenance, trained | 1.6 | Morton 2018 hypertrophy plateau |
| Standard cut | 1.8–2.2 | Phillips/Helms consensus |
| Aggressive cut, lean (<12% BF) | 2.2–2.4 | Up to 3.1 g/kg FFM ceiling |
| Older lifter (>50) | 2.0–2.4 | Anabolic resistance offset |

CutTrack's recommendation engine encodes this as the **R-protein-floor**
rule: **1.8 g/kg total BW** is a hard minimum any deficit plan must
satisfy. Plans projecting intake below the floor are rejected.

## Distribution

Total daily protein matters most, but distribution amplifies MPS
[^schoenfeld2018][^moore2009]:

- **3–5 feedings/day**, spaced ~3–5 hours apart.
- **0.4–0.55 g/kg per meal** to maximise the per-bout MPS response.
- **Leucine ≥2.5–3 g per feeding** to clear the MPS-trigger threshold.
  Whey, dairy, eggs, and ~150 g cooked lean meat all clear this dose.

Below ~0.24 g/kg per meal, the MPS response is sub-maximal — common in
"protein once a day" patterns and the main reason high totals from a
single meal underperform spread intake.

## Pre-sleep protein

30–40 g of casein 30 minutes before bed elevates overnight MPS
[^snijders2015]. The effect is **small once the daily target is met**;
treat it as optimisation, not a requirement. Useful when the last meal
ends >4 hours before sleep.

## Worked example — 96 kg cutting lifter

- **Daily target: 175–200 g** (≈1.8–2.1 g/kg total BW).
- **4–5 meals**, each **38–48 g** protein.
- Each meal contributes ≥2.5 g leucine.
- Optional 30–40 g casein pre-sleep.

If body composition is known (e.g., 15% BF → FFM ≈ 81 kg), 195 g/day is
~2.4 g/kg FFM — comfortably inside Helms' 2.3–3.1 band [^helms2014].

## What this rules out

- **<1.6 g/kg in a deficit** — Longland-grade evidence shows lean-mass
  loss accelerates.
- **Single-meal protein loading** — wastes MPS capacity, hurts satiety
  spread.
- **Unlimited protein "the more the better"** — beyond ~3.1 g/kg FFM the
  marginal MPS gain disappears and displaces carbs/fat needed for
  training quality.

## Engine rules

- `R-protein-floor`: minimum **1.8 g/kg total BW** in any cut plan.
- `R-protein-ceiling`: **2.4 g/kg total BW** or **3.1 g/kg FFM** if
  composition known.
- `R-meal-distribution`: ≥3 feedings, each ≥0.4 g/kg.
- Pairs with `rate_of_loss_lean_retention` (deficit size) and
  `refeeds_diet_breaks` (sustainability).

## Confidence

**High.** Five independent Tier-1 sources converge on the 1.6–2.4 g/kg
range, with Longland providing direct RCT evidence in a deficit. Open
questions (plant-protein adjustment, extreme deficits >30%) are flagged
in the raw note.
