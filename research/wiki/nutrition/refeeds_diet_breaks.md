# Refeeds & Diet Breaks

> Run weekly carb-led refeed days on hard training days plus one 7-day diet break at maintenance around week 6 — refeeds protect lean mass and adherence, not fat-loss speed.

## TL;DR
Refeeds and diet breaks **don't accelerate fat loss** — they protect lean mass, RMR, training quality, and adherence. For a 12-week trained-lifter cut, run **weekly carb-led refeed days on hard training days** (Campbell 2020) and schedule **one 7-day diet break at maintenance around week 6** (MATADOR / Trexler).

## What's a refeed vs a diet break
| | Refeed | Diet break |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | 1–2 days | 7–14 days |
| Kcal | Maintenance | True maintenance |
| Macros | Carbs ↑↑, fat ↓, protein constant | Normal split |
| Frequency | 1–2x/week | Every 6–12 wks of deficit |
| Primary mechanism | Leptin + glycogen + training quality | RMR/NEAT recovery + adherence |

## The evidence

**MATADOR (Byrne 2018)** — obese men, 16 weeks of restriction. The 2-weeks-on / 2-weeks-off arm lost **~50% more fat mass** (14.1 kg vs 9.1 kg) than continuous-deficit and preserved RMR closer to baseline [^matador]. Cleanest evidence that periodic maintenance breaks blunt adaptive thermogenesis.

**Campbell 2020** — resistance-trained men, 7-week 25% deficit. Two consecutive maintenance/refeed days per week (carbs elevated, weekly kcal matched) preserved **slightly more lean mass and FFMI** vs continuous; **fat loss was equal** [^campbell2020]. Refeeds preserve, they don't accelerate.

**Trexler 2014** — metabolic adaptation review. Deficit depth × duration drives RMR drop, NEAT drop, leptin drop, T3 drop. Carbohydrate-led refeeds and periodic diet breaks attenuate the cascade [^trexler2014].

**Peos 2019** — systematic review of intermittent vs continuous restriction. Intermittent ≥ continuous on fat loss at matched cumulative deficit; **adherence is the most consistent advantage** [^peos2019].

## Why it works (when it works)
1. Carbs raise leptin within hours; fat does not — refeeds must be carb-led
2. Glycogen replenishment protects training output → MPS stimulus → lean mass
3. T3 and NEAT recover (partially) at maintenance kcal
4. Planned high-carb days reduce binge risk and disordered drift — adherence is the dominant practical lever

## Why it doesn't speed fat loss
Maintenance days reduce cumulative deficit. The math only breaks even when preserved RMR/NEAT and training quality offset the lost deficit-days. Campbell (7 wks) saw a wash on fat loss; MATADOR (16+ deficit wks) saw it tip toward intermittent because adaptive thermogenesis had time to compound in the continuous arm.

## Refeed protocol
- **When**: 1–2x/wk, on heaviest training day
- **Kcal**: at TDEE (maintenance), not surplus
- **Carbs**: ~5–7 g/kg
- **Fat**: held low (<0.8 g/kg)
- **Protein**: constant (~2.2 g/kg)
- **Don't**: lower protein, raise fat, alcohol, treat as cheat day

## Diet-break protocol
- **When**: every 6–12 wks of continuous deficit
- **Duration**: 7–14 days
- **Kcal**: true maintenance (recompute TDEE)
- **Macros**: normal split — no carb spike required (different mechanism than refeed)
- **Training**: maintain; don't deload unless overdue
- **Expect**: 0.5–1.5 kg scale gain from glycogen + gut content. Not fat regain.

## Recommendation for a 12-week cut
1. **Weekly training-day carb-cycle high day** — Campbell 2020 protocol; small lean-mass edge plus training-quality benefit. CutTrack's existing carb-cycle "training day high" already implements this.
2. **One 7-day diet break at week 6** at true maintenance — MATADOR / Trexler protocol; resets leptin, T3, NEAT before the second half of the cut.
3. **Between phases** (if stacking 12-week cuts) — mandatory 7–14 day diet break before re-entering deficit.

Skip the refeed/diet-break complexity if the cut is short (<6 wks) or body fat is high — the metabolic-adaptation case is weakest there.

## Engine integration
- Carb-cycle high days already align with refeed evidence (T1 support)
- Add scheduled **7-day diet break between cut phases**, exempted from rate-of-loss guardrails (zero loss is the goal)
- After diet break, allow **3–5 days of trend re-stabilization** before reacting to rate signals — glycogen/water rebound will distort the EMA

## Limits
- MATADOR was obese, not lean lifters
- Campbell was 7 weeks — short for full adaptive thermogenesis effects
- No RCT of "12-week trained cut with vs without mid-cut diet break" exists
- Refeeds don't rescue an over-aggressive deficit; pair with sane rate-of-loss (0.5–1%/wk)
