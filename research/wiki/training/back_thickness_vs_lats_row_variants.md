# Back Thickness vs Lats — Row and Pulldown Variant Selection

> "Thickness" vs "width" is a coaching mnemonic, not anatomy. The real distinction: vertical pulls bias the lat in its lengthened position; horizontal pulls bias the mid-back and thoracic-region lat in shortened positions. Train both, prefer chest-supported rows, and bias machines that allow full lat lengthening.

## What the evidence says

The bodybuilding "thickness vs width" split — rows for thickness, pulldowns for width — is folk taxonomy. Both movement patterns hit the lats and both hit the mid-back, but at different positions on the length-tension curve and with different mechanical advantages for different fibre regions[^lehman-2004][^ronai-2015][^beardsley-lat].

The lat is a fan-shaped muscle with one tendon and a wide origin. Its thoracic (upper) fibers are mechanically advantaged for shoulder extension at short-to-moderate lengths — the position they reach during a row. Its lumbar/iliac (lower) fibers are mechanically advantaged for shoulder adduction at moderate-to-long lengths — the position they reach during a pulldown or pull-up[^ronai-2015][^park-2014][^hdemg-2025].

Mid-back muscles — mid and lower trapezius, rhomboids, posterior deltoid — are scapular retractors and humeral horizontal abductors. They are loaded most when the elbow travels behind the torso plane, which is the defining feature of horizontal pulling[^lehman-2004][^fenwick-2009].

Lehman 2004 found the highest lat-to-biceps activation ratio during the wide-grip pulldown and the seated row, and the highest mid-trap/rhomboid activation during the seated row[^lehman-2004]. Fenwick 2009 found the inverted row produced the highest lat and upper-back activation while the bent-over barbell row produced the highest lumbar erector load and spine compression — i.e., bent rows tax the lower back more than they need to for the same back stimulus[^fenwick-2009]. Grip width and forearm orientation produce small, mostly inconsequential differences in lat EMG; pronated grips slightly favour lats over biceps[^andersen-2014][^mdpi-2025-pulldown][^lehman-2004].

EMG measures acute neural drive, not hypertrophy. Treat all EMG findings as suggestive, not conclusive[^vigotsky-2018]. The hypertrophy framework is anchored on mechanical tension at long muscle lengths and hard sets near failure[^wolf-2023-rom][^maeo-2022-lengthened][^refalo-2023-rir].

Chest-supported row variants reduce lower-back fatigue, eliminate cheating momentum, and let the lifter push closer to failure on multiple working sets[^fenwick-2009][^bbm-csr]. They are the better hard-set driver for hypertrophy. Bent-over barbell rows are still useful for heavy strength work but are dominated by chest-supported alternatives for pure back hypertrophy, especially for taller lifters where torso position is harder to hold[^bbm-csr][^fenwick-2009].

Cable rows with neutral grip and a slight forward lean at the lengthened end give a long lat stretch and full shoulder extension at the contracted end. Grip width and orientation shift activation slightly (narrow neutral biases lats; wide pronated biases traps and rear delts) but effect sizes are small[^sbs-rowing][^seated-row-grip-2023].

The Wolf 2023 / Maeo 2022 lengthened-position bias generalises poorly without thought to the lat. The lat operates near the plateau of its length-tension curve per Gerling 2013, so it may be somewhat less stretch-responsive than triceps or hamstrings — but the directional advice (load it long, not clipped) still holds[^wolf-2023-rom][^maeo-2022-lengthened][^gerling-2013].

## Practical rules

- Run **1 vertical pull + 1 horizontal pull per back session** as hard-set drivers; treat them as roughly co-equal[^rp-back][^3dmj-hypertrophy].
- Prefer **chest-supported row variants** over bent-over barbell rows for hypertrophy work — the lower-back fatigue savings convert directly into more hard sets near failure[^fenwick-2009][^bbm-csr].
- Bias **lengthened-position work**: full overhead reach on pulldowns, forward lean at the bottom of cable rows, full shoulder extension at the top of pullovers[^wolf-2023-rom][^maeo-2022-lengthened].
- Pick **machines and cables that allow your full reach** over fixed-bar lat pulldowns, especially if you are 6'+ — bar clearance limits ROM[^conor-harris-pulldown].
- Use **pronated grip** for slightly more lat bias on pulldowns; **neutral grip** with forward lean on cable rows for a long-stretch lat-biased horizontal pull[^andersen-2014][^seated-row-grip-2023].
- Don't chase grip-width minutiae; differences are small[^lehman-2004][^andersen-2014].
- Push compounds to RIR 1–3, isolations to RIR 0–1[^refalo-2023-rir].

## Recommended pair for a 6'2" commercial-gym session

**Vertical pull driver:** Single-arm half-kneeling cable lat pulldown — or, if available, a chest-supported / iso-lateral plate-loaded high row (Hammer Strength). Both let a long-armed lifter reach full lat lengthening without the bar bottoming out on thighs, and the iso-lateral version forces equal work per side. Avoid standard fixed-bar lat pulldowns when thigh-pad clearance limits ROM.

**Horizontal pull driver:** Chest-supported T-bar row (or chest-supported dumbbell row on a 30°–45° incline bench). Removes the lower back as a fatigue limiter, holds torso angle constant, allows long ROM without lumbar compromise, and lets multiple sets stay near failure. If a Hammer Strength iso-lateral row is available, it is the cleanest single answer.

Optional accessories: cable straight-arm pulldown or rope cable pullover for direct lat stretch work; rope face pull or reverse pec deck for rear delts and lower trap.

## What this does not say

This does not say bent-over barbell rows are useless — they remain a strong full-body strength stimulus and a posterior-chain co-trainer. It says they are dominated by chest-supported variants when the goal is back hypertrophy under a given systemic-fatigue budget[^fenwick-2009][^bbm-csr].

This does not say grip choice is irrelevant. It says the differences are small and that grip should be picked for joint comfort and stretch quality, not for marginal EMG advantages[^lehman-2004][^andersen-2014][^sbs-rowing].

This does not say "thickness" and "width" are independently trainable to a meaningful degree. Regional EMG differences exist but regional hypertrophy from row vs pulldown bias has not been demonstrated in long-block RCTs[^park-2014][^hdemg-2025].

This does not say one vertical + one horizontal is a magic prescription. It is a sensible default; lifters with weak point biases (clearly underdeveloped lats vs clearly underdeveloped mid-back) should skew accordingly within a microcycle[^rp-back].

This does not say the lengthened-bias literature transfers cleanly to the lat. Architectural analysis suggests the lat operates near plateau; the "load it long" rule still holds directionally, but the upside may be smaller than for triceps or hamstrings[^gerling-2013][^wolf-2023-rom].
