# Mechanical Tension and Hypertrophy

> Mechanical tension on actively contracting fibers is the primary driver of hypertrophy; metabolic stress and damage are mostly modulators or epiphenomena.

## What the evidence says

The "three mechanisms" model from Schoenfeld 2010 — tension, metabolic stress, muscle damage — has been progressively narrowed[^schoenfeld-2010-mechanisms]. Wackerhage et al. 2019 reframed hypertrophy as a mechanotransductive response to high tension on working fibers, with metabolic and damage signals largely co-varying with tension rather than acting independently[^wackerhage-2019]. Roberts et al. 2023 confirms mTORC1, activated by load-sensitive mechanosensors, as the central anabolic switch[^roberts-2023-mps].

Tension comes in two forms. Active tension is force generated by cross-bridge cycling under load. Passive tension is force borne by titin and the extracellular matrix at long muscle lengths. Both feed into the same anabolic signaling, which is why training muscles in a lengthened position produces robust hypertrophy and why lengthened partials can match or modestly exceed full-ROM work[^wolf-2023-rom][^maeo-2022-lengthened][^kassiano-2023-rom].

Effective mechanical tension on a fiber is highest when (a) the motor unit containing it is recruited and (b) it is contracting slowly under load. Both conditions are satisfied near the end of a hard set, when high-threshold motor units have been progressively recruited and bar speed has dropped[^morton-2019-effort]. This is why proximity to failure matters: the last few reps before failure are where most of the hypertrophic stimulus lives[^refalo-2023-rir].

Because effective tension is recruitment-gated, the rep range is largely interchangeable as long as sets are taken near failure. Meta-analyses across loading zones show similar hypertrophy from roughly 30% to 85% 1RM when sets reach RIR 0–3[^schoenfeld-2017-loads][^lopez-2021-meta]. This is the empirical basis for treating the hard set near failure as the unit of stimulus[^baz-valle-2022-volume].

Damage is not anabolic on its own. Damas et al. show hypertrophy proceeds, and accelerates, as damage subsides across a training block[^damas-2018-damage]. Metabolic stress likely matters only insofar as it forces recruitment of high-threshold units in lower-load conditions[^schoenfeld-2013-metab][^wackerhage-2019].

## Practical rules

- Count weekly hard sets per muscle (RIR 0–3) as your volume metric[^baz-valle-2022-volume][^refalo-2023-rir].
- Use any rep range from 5 to 30 — pick by joint tolerance and exercise stability, not by mythical "hypertrophy zones"[^schoenfeld-2017-loads][^lopez-2021-meta].
- Prefer exercises that load the target muscle in a stretched position when joint health allows[^maeo-2022-lengthened][^wolf-2023-rom].
- Push compounds to RIR 1–3, isolation work closer to RIR 0[^refalo-2023-rir].
- Do not chase soreness or pump as proof of stimulus — neither tracks growth well[^damas-2018-damage].
- During a cut, preserve effective tension: keep load high, reduce set count rather than reducing intensity[^refalo-2023-rir][^baz-valle-2022-volume].

## What this does not say

This does not say metabolic stress and damage are useless — only that they are not independent levers and chasing them as goals (lots of pump work, lots of eccentric overload solely for damage) is not better than just generating high tension on hard sets[^damas-2018-damage][^wackerhage-2019].

This does not say all rep ranges feel the same or have the same fatigue cost. 30-rep sets of squats are far more cardiovascularly punishing than 6-rep sets, even if hypertrophy is similar; load and rep range still matter for fatigue management[^mass-loads-2022].

This does not say load is irrelevant. Loads under ~30% 1RM appear to underperform even at failure, likely because peripheral fatigue and discomfort end the set before motor-unit recruitment is maximal[^lopez-2021-meta][^mass-loads-2022].

This does not say more hard sets are always better. Tension-based stimulus still obeys diminishing returns and recoverable-volume ceilings — covered separately under volume landmarks.

This does not extend beyond resistance training. Tension-driven hypertrophy is the model for traditional weight training; sarcoplasmic hypertrophy via blood-flow-restricted or extreme-metabolite work is a related but distinct topic[^schoenfeld-2013-metab].
