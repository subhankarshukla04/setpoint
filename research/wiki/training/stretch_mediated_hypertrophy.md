# Stretch-Mediated Hypertrophy

> Loading a muscle at long lengths grows it more than loading it at short lengths, at matched volume. The effect is biggest on biarticular muscles and on muscles conventional training rarely stretches.

## What the evidence says

The Wolf et al. 2023 meta-analysis on partial vs full ROM found only a trivial overall edge for full ROM (SMD 0.12, 95% CI -0.02 to 0.26)[^wolf-2023]. The variable that actually mattered was where in the ROM the load was applied: when partials were performed in the lengthened end, hypertrophy matched or exceeded full-ROM training[^wolf-2023]. A more recent regional meta-analysis returned similar small SMDs across muscle sites (proximal 0.04, mid 0.07, distal 0.09)[^wolf-2025], reinforcing that "ROM" is the wrong abstraction — muscle length under load is the right one.

Direct long- vs short-length comparisons show much larger effects. Kassiano et al. found medial gastrocnemius grew significantly more with long-length partials than with full ROM or short-length partials at matched volume[^kassiano-2023]. Maeo et al. found overhead triceps extensions grew the triceps 19.9% vs 13.5% for neutral-arm extensions[^maeo-2023]; the long head, biarticular and only fully stretched in shoulder flexion, drove the gap. Maeo et al. also found seated (hip-flexed) leg curls grew the hamstrings 14.1% vs 9.3% lying[^maeo-2021], with all three biarticular heads benefiting. Pedrosa et al. concluded across the literature that "exercises performed in a lengthened position resulted in greater muscle hypertrophy."[^pedrosa-2023]

The effect is **not uniform across muscles**. It is largest on **biarticular** muscles — triceps long head, hamstrings, rectus femoris, biceps long head, lats — where one joint position can dramatically change muscle length, and where a conventional exercise often fails to reach the deep stretch. Monoarticular muscles still respond, but the within-muscle ceiling is lower because the lengthened end is easier to reach by default.

Stronger By Science frames the mechanism cautiously: "tension at long lengths" rather than a separate stretch signalling pathway[^sbs-lengthened]. Practical implication is the same either way.

## Practical rules

1. **Bias exercise selection toward the lengthened version** at matched sets. The free win is upgrading the exercise, not adding volume.
2. **Use these defaults per muscle group:**
   - Hamstrings — seated leg curl, deficit RDL.
   - Quads — ATG / front squat, deep leg press; not quarter squats.
   - Triceps — overhead extension (DB, cable rope, EZ-bar); pushdown only as accessory.
   - Biceps — incline DB curl (long head); preacher for brachialis bias.
   - Chest — deep DB press or DB fly with full stretch; deficit push-up; not floor press for hypertrophy.
   - Lats — pullover, kneeling lat prayer, snatch-grip pulldown with full reach.
   - Calves — loaded stretch on a raised platform; pause at the bottom.
   - Glutes — deficit RDL, B-stance hip thrust at full hip flexion, ATG split squat.
3. **Lengthened partials are a tool, not a requirement.** When a full ROM is comfortable and the load profile reaches the stretch, just do full ROM. Reach for long-length partials when (a) the lifter is fatigued mid-set and the lengthened end is the still-effective portion, or (b) the exercise mechanically loads the stretch hardest.
4. **Pause briefly in the stretched position** on at least one work set per exercise — it locks in the lengthened-position stimulus and disambiguates "did I actually stretch under load."
5. **Don't trade volume for stretch.** The evidence is at matched volume. Long-length bias upgrades the same sets; it does not let you cut sets.

## What this does not say

- **Stretching alone does not replace lifting.** Static-stretch hypertrophy effects in humans are small (d ~0.20)[^sbs-lengthened] — orders of magnitude below resistance training. The animal-study claims (SMD ~7.9) do not transfer.
- **More ROM is not always better.** The win is from loading at long lengths, not from grinding through a painful or unstable end-range. If joint position is painful, the muscle isn't getting the stimulus you think it is.
- **Some setups quietly cancel the effect** — small-ROM machines that bottom out short, banded/cable profiles where peak resistance lives in the shortened position, partial benches that stop above the chest. In these, the loaded stretch is never reached and the long-length advantage collapses.
- **Not every muscle benefits equally.** Muscles where conventional training already reaches the stretch (e.g., delts in lateral raises with full ROM, forearms in most curls) show smaller gains from "more stretch" interventions.
- **Mechanism is unsettled.** Whether this is true stretch-mediated signalling or just mechanical tension applied at long lengths is debated; current evidence does not require choosing.
