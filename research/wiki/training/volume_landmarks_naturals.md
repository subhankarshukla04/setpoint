# Volume Landmarks for Natural Lifters

> Hypertrophy scales with weekly hard sets per muscle (12–20 is the sweet spot for most groups), with diminishing returns; the RP MEV/MAV/MRV framework operationalises that curve into actionable starting numbers.

## What the evidence says
Weekly hard-set volume is the dominant driver of hypertrophy once a minimum stimulus is met. Schoenfeld's 2017 meta-analysis pooled 15 studies and reported a graded dose-response of roughly 0.37% additional muscle growth per added weekly set, with the 10+ sets/wk tier outperforming <5[^schoenfeld-2017-volume]. Baz-Valle's 2022 systematic review of trained young men converged on 12–20 hard sets per muscle per week as the optimal range[^bazvalle-2022-volume]. The most recent meta-regression (Pelland et al. 2025, 67 studies, 2058 subjects) confirms the slope is positive with 100% posterior probability while showing pronounced diminishing returns and emphasising that direct *and* indirect sets must be counted to predict adaptation accurately[^pelland-2025-doseresp].

A "hard set" in this literature is a working set taken within ~3 reps of technical failure (RIR ≤ 3, RPE ≥ 7). Sets further from failure either don't count or are discounted, because proximity-to-failure non-linearly drives hypertrophy[^refalo-2023-proximity]. Frequency, when total volume is equated, has small-to-negligible effects on growth — Schoenfeld 2019 found no meaningful frequency benefit beyond ≥2x/wk per muscle, a finding upheld by Pelland 2025[^schoenfeld-2019-frequency][^pelland-2025-doseresp]. Splitting volume across more sessions matters mainly because it lets you accumulate more quality hard sets before single-session fatigue degrades them.

The Renaissance Periodization framework (Israetel/Hoffmann) maps the dose-response curve onto four landmarks: MV (maintenance), MEV (minimum effective), MAV (the productive range), MRV (maximum recoverable)[^rp-landmarks]. Stronger By Science cautions that even strong group means hide ~30–50% individual variance — some lifters grow on 6 sets/wk, others need 25+[^nuckols-volume]. The framework is best treated as a calibrated starting point, not a prescription. Heaselgrave 2019 (n=49 trained men, biceps) is a notable dissent: 9, 18, and 27 weekly sets produced statistically equivalent growth, supporting an inverted-U for small muscles[^heaselgrave-2019-biceps].

## Practical rules
- Count only sets within RIR ≤ 3 of technical failure as "hard sets"; lighter sets don't move you up the dose-response curve.
- Per-muscle starting numbers (intermediate natural, hard sets/wk)[^rp-landmarks]:
  - Chest 8 / 10 / 12–20 / 22 (MV/MEV/MAV/MRV)
  - Back 8 / 10 / 14–22 / 25
  - Side delts 6 / 8 / 16–22 / 26 — Rear delts 0 / 6 / 12–18 / 22
  - Biceps 4 / 6 / 14–20 / 26 — Triceps 4 / 6 / 10–14 / 18
  - Quads 6 / 8 / 12–18 / 20 — Hamstrings 4 / 6 / 10–16 / 20
  - Glutes 0 / 0 / 4–12 / 16 — Calves 4 / 6 / 8–16 / 20
- Start each mesocycle at MEV; add ~1 set/wk per muscle; aim to be in MAV by mid-block; deload when MRV signs appear.
- Distribute volume across ≥2 sessions/wk per muscle once weekly hard sets exceed 8–10. Frequency above that is a recovery/scheduling tool, not a growth lever[^schoenfeld-2019-frequency].
- Treat the table as ±30–50%. Trust the lifter's progress over the table after 2–3 mesocycles[^nuckols-volume].
- Detect "above MRV" empirically — deload when any 2 of these 3 hit:
  - Performance drift (load × reps drops session-over-session despite adequate sleep/food).
  - Recovery deficit (persistent joint ache, sleep disruption, elevated resting HR, motivation collapse).
  - Pump and session quality decay despite rising nominal volume.

## What this does not say
- These numbers are population averages from trained, drug-free, young-adult men. Beginners grow at or below MV; advanced lifters often need higher volumes. Cuts compress MRV — expect 20–30% less recoverable volume in a deficit.
- "Junk volume" — light sets at RIR ≥ 4, no overload, sloppy technique — does not count toward MEV/MAV. Inflating set counts with junk does not unlock more growth.
- Volume is a *moderator*, not the whole story. Without near-failure intensity, mechanical-tension quality, and progressive overload, even MAV-level set counts underperform.
- The flattening point (~10–20 sets/wk) is where curves bend, not stop. The literature shows diminishing returns; a strict negative slope above MRV is mostly inferred from practitioner observation, not proven in short RCTs.
- Heaselgrave 2019 contests the curve shape for small muscles. Treat biceps and triceps recommendations as the most uncertain band of the table[^heaselgrave-2019-biceps].
